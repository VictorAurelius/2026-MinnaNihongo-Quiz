# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Smart Quiz primarily serves Vietnamese learners aged 18–35 who study Japanese (JLPT N5–N1) or Chinese (HSK1–5). They typically study independently at home or while commuting, on mobile devices, in focused sessions of 10–30 minutes.

Their core job is to understand and retain vocabulary, kanji, grammar, readings, and pronunciation through active recall and spaced repetition. The interface must make the next learning action obvious without competing with the language content.

## Product Purpose

Smart Quiz is an offline-first bilingual language-learning application. It makes structured Japanese and Chinese course content available through flashcards, multiple-choice quizzes, typing practice, review, and progress tracking without requiring a network connection or backend.

Success means learners can start a study session quickly, remain focused, understand mistakes, and return with confidence that their progress is preserved.

## Brand Personality

**Encouraging, clean, smart.** The product speaks like a supportive tutor: knowledgeable and approachable, never condescending. It celebrates success, responds gently to mistakes, and remains calm during concentrated study.

The desired emotional outcome is steady confidence. Learners should feel that difficult material is manageable rather than simplified or trivialized.

## Anti-references

- Cluttered education apps with too many competing colors, controls, and promotions.
- Gamified products that optimize entertainment at the expense of actual learning.
- Generic AI aesthetics: purple-to-blue gradients, glowing dark surfaces, glassmorphism, nested cards, and decorative icon tiles.
- Interfaces that hide learning content behind ornamental layout or excessive animation.
- Desktop-first study flows that become cramped or indirect on mobile.

## Design Principles

1. **Content is the product.** Vocabulary, kanji, grammar, examples, and quiz prompts always own the visual hierarchy.
2. **One obvious next action.** Each study state should make the learner's next step unmistakable.
3. **Feedback builds confidence.** Correct, incorrect, loading, empty, offline, and completion states must be immediate and constructive.
4. **Progressive disclosure protects focus.** Advanced controls appear when useful, not all at once.
5. **Offline is the default experience.** Core learning flows never depend on a network response.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and interaction requirements.
- Preserve semantic HTML, visible focus states, screen-reader labels, and keyboard navigation.
- Keep touch targets at least 44×44 px in mobile study flows.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; motion communicates state and never gates content.
- Preserve quiz shortcuts: F1 for speech, Space/Enter for flashcards, and 1–4 for multiple choice.
- Render Japanese and Chinese scripts at comfortable sizes with appropriate CJK font stacks and line height.
